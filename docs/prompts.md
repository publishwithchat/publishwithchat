# Getting a good website from your chatbot

The chatbot builds the pages, so the more it knows about your business, the better the first preview. You do not need technical words.

## What to tell it

- **Who you are:** business name, what you do, where you are.
- **What people need to find:** menu or services, prices, opening hours, contact details, a map.
- **How it should feel:** friendly, formal, modern, traditional. A colour or two if you have them.
- **Your own material:** attach your logo and photos and ask it to use them exactly as they are.

## A good first request

```
Make me a website for [your business name]. Include [what you want, like a menu,
prices, opening hours, contact details and a map]. Use my attached logo and photos
exactly as they are. Publish it with Publish with chat and give me the preview link.
```

## Short versions

- `@Publish with chat build a one-page website for my plumbing business and show me a preview`
- `Make a website for my bakery and publish it with Publish with chat`

## Changing a site you already have

Ask in plain words. You need to be signed in to Publish with chat for this.

- `Change my opening hours to 9 to 5 on weekdays and publish the update`
- `Replace the photo on the home page with the one I just attached`
- `Add a page with our price list`

## Going back

Every update is kept as a version.

- `Show me the earlier versions of my site`
- `Switch my site back to the version from yesterday`

## Good to know

- You get a free preview link that lasts 48 hours. Preview files are kept for 14 days, so you can still put the site online after the link stops working.
- Sites are static: pages, images, styles and small scripts. There is no server code or database.
- If the chatbot says a page has a problem, ask it to fix it and send the site again. Publish with chat checks every upload and tells the chatbot what is wrong.
